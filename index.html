Remeder Privacy Policy

Last updated 20 October 2026

Remeder is a medication reminder app. It stores the medicines you choose to track, for yourself or for someone in your care, reminds you when a dose is due, and keeps a record of the doses you mark as taken or skipped. It can read a medicine's label from a photo, and it lets you share a person's medicines with someone who helps look after them. This policy explains what the app collects, where it goes, and how you can remove it.

Remeder is not a medical device. It does not provide medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment, and it is not a substitute for a pharmacist or doctor. Always follow the directions on your prescription.

Who is responsible for your data

Remeder is operated by the developer of the app, acting as the data controller for the information described below. Contact details are at the bottom of this page.

What Remeder collects

Everything below is information you enter or generate by using the app. Remeder has no advertising, no analytics SDKs, no third-party trackers, and no crash-reporting SDK. It does not buy or receive data about you from anyone else.

Data that stays only on your device

  • Reminders. Dose reminders are scheduled by your phone's own alarm and notification system. The content of a reminder is not sent to any server.
  • Label photos read on the phone, which is the default. They are deleted when you finish the scan.
  • The on-device reading model, once downloaded.
  • Cached data and preferences, held in local app storage so the app works without a connection.
  • Biometric app lock. If you enable it, the check is performed by your phone. Remeder never receives your fingerprint or face data.

What Remeder does not collect

  • No location data. A label photo that is sent to be read leaves without the location and camera details the phone stored in it.
  • No contacts or files, and no photos other than those you take or choose for a scan.
  • No audio. Remeder does not use the microphone.
  • No advertising identifiers, and no data is used for advertising or sold to anyone.

Where your data is stored

Your account and the data listed above are stored in Google Firebase (Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore), operated by Google LLC, on Google's servers. Data is encrypted in transit and encrypted at rest by Google. Security rules limit each signed-in account to its own records. What someone you share a person with can see is served to them by Remeder's server, which shows them only what is described under Sharing a person.

Remeder's server runs on Google Cloud in the United States. It relays label photos for cloud reading, runs sharing, and sends check-in alerts. It has no database of its own and does not store photos. Like any web service it keeps request logs — the time, the network address a request came from, which part of the service was asked for, and for sharing, the account and person it concerned — which Google Cloud deletes after 30 days.

Google acts as a processor for this data. See Firebase's privacy documentation for details of their handling. Your data may therefore be processed in countries other than your own, including the United States.

Prescription scanning

You can add a medicine by photographing its label or package. A label photo can show the patient's name, the hospital or pharmacy, and the medicine, so Remeder reads it on your phone unless you choose otherwise.

On your phone (the default)

An AI model on your phone reads the photo, and the photo never leaves the phone. The model is downloaded once, from Hugging Face (2.6 GB, or 3.7 GB for the larger model you can choose). That download is an ordinary file request: Hugging Face sees your network address, as any website would, and nothing about you or your medicines.

In the cloud (only if you choose it)

If you choose Cloud AI in Label Processing, or allow the cloud to read a label when your phone cannot, the photos are sent to Remeder's server, which passes them to Cloudflare Workers AI. There, Gemma — an openly published AI model, run by Cloudflare on its own hardware — reads them and returns the medicine's details.

  • Before a photo is sent, the location and camera details stored in it are removed.
  • Remeder's server does not keep the photos or what was read from them.
  • Cloudflare's Workers AI terms say it does not retain what it is sent after the request is answered, and does not use it to train AI models, except where the law requires it to retain something.
  • The photos are not sent to Google or to any other AI company.

Whichever way a label is read, you check every value before the medicine is added, and the photos on your phone are deleted when you finish.

Sharing a person

You can let someone who helps look after a person — a family member or a carer — see that person's medicines from their own Remeder account.

  • How it starts. You create an invitation, confirming that the person has agreed to it, and send the link or code yourself. An invitation expires after 24 hours unless accepted. Access lasts 90 days unless you choose otherwise (at most a year), and at most five accounts can have access to one person.
  • What they see. The person's name, avatar, and colour, and their doses from 14 days back to 7 days ahead: each medicine's name, when it is due, whether it was confirmed, and who recorded it. They do not see the person's date of birth, allergies, conditions, or notes, nor stock or anyone else on your account.
  • What they can do. A viewer can only look. A caregiver can also record a dose as taken, skipped, or not taken, which appears in your records with their name.
  • Check-in alerts. Someone with access can turn on alerts. If a dose has not been confirmed some time after it was due, their phone gets a notification naming the person and the dose time — not the medicine. A record of each alert is kept for 30 days so it is not sent twice.
  • What you see about them. Their name and email address, their role, and when their access began and ends.
  • Ending it. You can remove one person's access, or everyone's, at any time, and someone given access can give it up.

Who your data is shared with

Remeder does not sell your data and does not share it for advertising. Data is disclosed only:

  • To Google, through Firebase and Google Cloud, as the hosting, sign-in, and notification provider described above.
  • To Cloudflare, only the label photos of a scan you chose to have read in the cloud, as described under Prescription scanning.
  • To people you share a person with, as described under Sharing a person.
  • To you, when you deliberately use an export or share feature — a calendar file, a Google Calendar event (which opens Google Calendar with the medicine and its schedule filled in), a PDF report, or a data export. You choose the recipient; Remeder does not send anything on its own.
  • Where the law requires it, such as a valid legal order.

How long it is kept

Your data is kept for as long as your account exists. Delete your account and it is removed, as described below, with these exceptions:

  • A record that your account was deleted, and when, is kept so the deletion can be completed and confirmed.
  • If someone had shared a person with you, the doses you recorded for that person stay in their history, under your name, because they are part of that person's medication record. Your access, with your name and email on it, is removed.
  • Server request logs are deleted after 30 days, and check-in alert records after 30 days.

Your choices and rights

  • See and edit everything in the app at any time.
  • Export your data from the app, so you have your own copy.
  • Delete individual records — a medicine, a course, or a whole profile.
  • Keep label photos on your phone by leaving Label Processing on its on-device default.
  • Stop sharing a person, or turn off check-in alerts, at any time.
  • Delete your account from Profile → Privacy & security. This asks you to sign in again to confirm, then removes your profiles, medicines, schedules, dose history, sharing and invitations, and push tokens along with the account itself, including your access to anyone shared with you. It cannot be undone.
  • Turn off reminders in the app, or revoke the notification and alarm permissions in your phone's settings.

Depending on where you live, you may also have the right to object to or restrict processing, to data portability, or to complain to your local data protection authority. Write to the contact address below and we will respond.

Permissions Remeder asks for

  • Notifications — to show dose reminders and check-in alerts.
  • Alarms & reminders (exact alarms) — so a reminder arrives at the dose time rather than whenever the system next wakes. If you decline it, reminders still work but may be delayed.
  • Full-screen notifications — so a critical reminder is visible on a locked screen.
  • Run at startup — to restore your pending reminders after the phone restarts.
  • Camera — to photograph a label. By default Remeder opens your phone's own camera app instead.
  • Biometrics — used only if you turn on the app lock.
  • Internet — to sign in, sync your data, download the reading model, and share.

Children

Remeder is not directed at children and is intended for users aged 18 and over. An adult may record medicines on behalf of a child in their care; that information belongs to the adult's account and is under their control. We do not knowingly collect data directly from children.

Testing releases

This version of Remeder is distributed for testing. Test builds can change quickly, and data recorded during testing may be reset between versions. Please do not rely on a test build as your only record of a medication schedule.

Changes to this policy
